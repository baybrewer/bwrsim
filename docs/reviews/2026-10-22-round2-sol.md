## New findings

R1, R2 and R5 arise in round 2 modeling changes. R3 is a remaining compressed-state port defect exposed by the expanded EOS range. R4 and R6 document the still-open parts of Sol N7 and Sol N2, rather than claiming those symptoms first appeared in this round.

## R1 — Trapped gas makes the vessel and IC exchange different steam energies

- **severity:** medium
- **area:** physics
- **file:line:** crates/bwr-core/src/vessel.rs:602
- **wrong:** `Vessel::sat()` uses total pressure, including the new noncondensable partial pressure. IC requests now correctly use coolant temperature (`sim.rs:403`), but vessel IC withdrawal still debits `sat_p(total_pressure).h_g`. Isolated sealed cold-scenario inventory heated to 150 C, MSIVs shut, no feed, all ICs open: vessel port saturation temperature is 161.715245 C, vessel debit is 2,759,301.041 J/kg and IC receipt is 2,745,919 J/kg. One accepted 0.05 s step loses 28,466.101 J from vessel-plus-IC-primary storage after core heat, vessel ambient loss and IC pool heat are counted; no model error occurs.
- **consequence:** The new trapped-gas operating path violates primary-loop energy conservation, even though the gas-free regression cases close. Other consumers of the same saturation helper also see a steam temperature inconsistent with the EOS temperature.
- **fix:** Separate total mechanical pressure from water-vapor saturation properties. Use `sat_t(eos.t)` for the equilibrium vapor mass/enthalpy ports, and pass the identical steam enthalpy into IC requests and the vessel debit. Add an integrated gas-bearing heatup/IC balance with independently summed inventories and external heat terms.

## R2 — Condenser relief loses its equilibrium closure near dryout

- **severity:** medium
- **area:** physics
- **file:line:** crates/bwr-core/src/bop.rs:572
- **wrong:** The relief formula assumes `E_eq(M-m,T)=E_eq(M,T)-m*h_f` for all remaining masses. That identity stops holding once remaining inventory cannot fill the shell with saturated vapor. The code nevertheless clamps relief mass and forces `t=t_relief`, without re-solving equilibrium or reporting an unsupported state. Isolated Condenser::new(false), initialized at relief temperature, CW off, 1 GW continuous heat and zero requested mass inflow/outflow, with its normal 30 kg/s makeup: at dt=0.05 s, step index 3424 leaves 64.123267 kg and T=105.164407 C; stored energy is 19.500748482 GJ versus 19.090676513 GJ reconstructed from its own reported liquid/vapor/metal state. The 410.071969 MJ discrepancy has energy_residual_j=0. At dt=1 s the node reaches zero water while retaining a 2.949555 GJ discrepancy.
- **consequence:** Continued loss-of-cooling/depletion calculations carry energy that the displayed thermodynamic state cannot contain and produce spurious relief rates. Boundary-counter conservation alone does not detect this invalid state.
- **fix:** Solve the pressure-relief mass/energy problem with the actual piecewise equilibrium function, bounding discharge by available vapor/evaporation. Re-solve after discharge and handle the all-vapor/dry-metal regime explicitly, or reject and roll back at that unsupported boundary. Test reconstructed state energy, pressure and finite inventory through depletion at production and refined timesteps.

## R3 — Compressed-liquid outlets still use saturated-liquid enthalpy

- **severity:** medium
- **area:** physics
- **file:line:** crates/bwr-core/src/vessel.rs:646
- **wrong:** The expanded compressed-liquid EOS uses Region 1 pressure-dependent internal energy, but every liquid outlet still uses h_liquid(T) or saturated h_f(T), including liquid discharged through the nominal steam/vent ports when solid. Isolated compressed vessel at 200 C / 50.000049 MPa with simultaneous 1 kg/s liquid and nominal steam withdrawal accepts the step with no error and exports 852,393.100 J/kg; IF97 Region 1 at that same state gives 875,311.210 J/kg, a 22,918.110 J/kg deficit.
- **consequence:** Pressurized inventory drain/relief trajectories use an enthalpy inconsistent with their newly modeled M/V/U state. Connected bookkeeping can still balance because it repeats the same physically wrong port value.
- **fix:** Derive liquid-port enthalpy from the compressed EOS state, using Region 1 at actual T/P with the same saturation-table anchoring used for internal energy. Apply it consistently to RWCU, solid-state steam-line discharge and liquid venting; add an independent pressure-dependent withdrawal fixture.

## R4 — Browser extraction telemetry counts stored feed heat as turbine extraction

- **severity:** medium
- **area:** contract
- **file:line:** ui/src/sim/mock.ts:675
- **wrong:** The newly populated extractionHeatMw equals feed flow times temperature rise rather than heat actually supplied by extraction steam. Executed browser FullPower -> tripTurbine -> 100 steps of 0.05 s reports turbine steam=0 kg/s but extractionHeatMw=275.367475, feed=354.860481 kg/s and feed temperature=225.2 C. Feed remains warm from the existing approximate heater model, so this positive heat delivery cannot be labeled current turbine extraction.
- **consequence:** Browser clients see hundreds of MW of alleged turbine extraction after the turbine steam path has shut, contradicting the engine field and obscuring heater rundown during a trip.
- **fix:** Compute extraction from available turbine steam with a bounded extraction enthalpy budget; account for heater storage separately when deriving outlet temperature. If browser storage is intentionally omitted, report a supported approximation tied to current turbine flow and make extraction exactly zero without that flow. Add trip/isolation telemetry transition checks.

## R5 — Browser sealed-gas pressure never evolves or contributes to total pressure

- **severity:** medium
- **area:** contract
- **file:line:** ui/src/sim/mock.ts:706
- **wrong:** Both gas fields update only while headVentOpen is true. Closing the vent freezes the displayed partial pressure, and total pressure remains the old saturation/atmospheric approximation without adding gas. Executed cold scenario, one vented step at 40 C, then seal and evaluate at 90 C: gas mass stays 110.624411 kg, gas partial pressure stays 0.099422655 MPa and total pressure stays 0.101 MPa. With the mock's fixed 100 m3 headspace and R=0.287 kJ/kg/K, the retained gas alone requires approximately 0.1153 MPa at 90 C, before adding vapor pressure.
- **consequence:** The newly required trapped-gas telemetry contradicts its own declared ideal-gas state and the engine API semantics; browser sealed heatup cannot reproduce the pressure response introduced in round 2.
- **fix:** Initialize gas inventory on scenario load, retain only its mass when sealed, recompute its partial pressure from current temperature/volume each step, and include that partial pressure in total pressure with the vapor contribution. Reopening the vent should equilibrate both fields. Test cold load, seal, heat and reopen transitions.

## R6 — Accepted steam delivery never reaches the published flow fields

- **severity:** medium
- **area:** contract
- **file:line:** crates/bwr-core/src/sim.rs:456
- **wrong:** Acceptance scaling updates local w_tcv/w_byp only; self.steam.w_tcv/w_bypass remain hydraulic requests and snapshot publishes those fields at lines 913/914. Executed FullPower with vessel mass set to V*rho_f*(1-1e-8), consistent energy/EOS and one accepted 0.05 s step: actual total main-steam delivery is 0.002000707 kg/s, while steamToTurbineKgs remains 467.478611 kg/s. Extraction is now correctly bounded, so the snapshot disagrees with its own turbine/condenser transfer.
- **consequence:** Operator instrumentation and next-step feedforward can describe hundreds of kg/s of flow that never left the vessel. The validation vessel-port checker likewise uses total_flow requests and will misattribute this mismatch when such a case is added.
- **fix:** Keep hydraulic requests separate from committed delivered flows. Commit the accepted TCV/bypass allocations before snapshots and delivered-flow feedback, retaining requested values only where explicitly needed by the hydraulic solve. Assert that published turbine-plus-bypass flow equals accepted vessel main-steam flow in vapor-limited and water-solid cases.
