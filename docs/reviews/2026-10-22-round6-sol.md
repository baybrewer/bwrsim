## New findings

All three concern the newly accepted bulk mixture flow. The fixtures are consistent accepted thermodynamic states, not assertions of reachability through a stock operator sequence. The gas-purge regression covers the bypass and inventory accounting; it does not exercise a gas-bearing turbine inlet or three-element feedforward. All severities are medium; sections are ordered by physical/control effect.

## R1 — Gas-bearing turbine inlet uses total pressure as pure-water pressure

- **severity:** medium
- **area:** physics
- **file:line:** crates/bwr-core/src/sim.rs:513
- **wrong:** The new split removes air from turbine mass flow, then passes the mixture's total chest pressure to the pure-water `TurbineInlet::new` phase and entropy calculations (`crates/bwr-core/src/bop.rs:477`). In the sealed 90 C/180000 kg water/100 kg air fixture, TCV=0.001 makes line loss only 0.005032 Pa. Chest total pressure is 229956.455 Pa, while water partial pressure is approximately 70182.358 Pa. The entering water is vapor at 2659529 J/kg, yet the inlet invents 2.426955% liquid solely from applying saturation at total pressure. Independent IF97 gives quality 1 at the water partial pressure and 0.975730 at the incorrectly substituted total pressure. This is an almost unchanged inlet state, so throttling cannot explain the invented liquid. Water phase equilibrium in air requires water partial pressure/fugacity, as described by [NIST's compressed-gas dew-point work](https://www.nist.gov/laboratories/tools-instruments/dew-point-measurements-water-compressed-gases).
- **consequence:** Gas-bearing turbine admission assigns false moisture and incorrect expansion work/extraction limits. The isolated inlet's per-kg work limit is 453639 J/kg using total pressure versus 309547 J/kg when the existing pure-water helper receives water partial pressures at the two ends. The latter comparison isolates the pressure substitution; it is not a complete mixture-turbine reference or a claimed plant power prediction.
- **fix:** Carry mixture composition into the turbine inlet, determine the water state from its partial pressure/fugacity and enthalpy, and calculate expansion consistently with the chosen mixture thermodynamics. Until that is supported, explicitly reject gas-bearing turbine admission while allowing the supported bypass gas transport. Add a near-zero line-loss mixed-gas inlet check that remains entirely vapor and an independently bounded expansion case.

## R2 — Three-element feedforward replaces departing air with feedwater

- **severity:** medium
- **area:** correctness
- **file:line:** crates/bwr-core/src/sim.rs:439
- **wrong:** `SteamSystem::total_flow()` now includes noncondensables (`:504`), but the next step still sends that entire quantity to `Feedwater::step_hydraulics` as water steam loss (`:470`). In the first accepted 90 C/180 t/100 kg air open-bypass step, the signal is 15.170391 kg/s, consisting of only 3.286540 kg/s water and 11.883851 kg/s air. At zero level error, with the same controller initialized to integral/FCV=0.5, the isolated three-element scan drives FCV to 0.505000 using this mixture signal versus 0.501503 using the actual departing water. The snapshot also labels all 15.170391 kg/s as steam-to-bypass while vessel steam loss is 3.286540 kg/s.
- **consequence:** Air purging causes false positive water-replacement demand and inconsistent steam-flow telemetry. Level feedback may eventually compensate, but the anticipatory three-element action is wrong during the purge.
- **fix:** Feed the previous accepted water-vapor main-line flow (`vessel.flows.w_steam`) to water level feedforward. Preserve total mixture flow for hydraulics and any instrument explicitly defined to measure it; expose or label gas/mixture flow separately instead of silently changing steam-only telemetry semantics. Add a coupled gas-bearing three-element test that holds water loss fixed while varying air coflow and verifies unchanged water feedforward demand.

## R3 — Mixed-gas valve conductance ignores composition even at fixed temperature

- **severity:** medium
- **area:** physics
- **file:line:** crates/bwr-core/src/bop.rs:136
- **wrong:** Both valve conductances remain fixed pure-steam constants; mixture density affects only line friction. Two accepted sealed 90 C/180 t fixtures with 0 and 100 kg air, TCV=0.001 and the same evacuated condenser give exactly the same normalized choked flow, `w/p_chest = 7.3857142857e-8 kg/(s Pa)`, despite different gas composition. Line drops are only 0.002163/0.005032 Pa. The 100 kg fixture is 78.335823% air by mass, giving an ideal-mixture gas constant of 324.810 J/(kg K) versus 461.526 for water vapor. Even holding gamma fixed, the choked `w/p` coefficient must change by `sqrt(461.526/324.810) = 1.192`; a calorically perfect estimate with cp_water=1900 and cp_air=1005 J/(kg K) gives 1.208. This follows from the independently published [NASA choked mass-flow relation](https://www1.grc.nasa.gov/beginners-guide-to-aeronautics/mass-flow-rate-equations/). Both fixtures have identical temperature, so this is separate from the acknowledged missing temperature correction.
- **consequence:** Moving air at the dome composition fixes species retention, but air-rich discharge capacity and purge/depressurization time still use a steam-only valve calibration. The ideal-gas estimate is a composition limiting check, not a calibrated BWRX-300 valve-capacity prediction.
- **fix:** Pass composition or mixture thermodynamic properties into the valve solve and apply a rated-normalized gas-constant/gamma correction to choked mass flux and the critical/subcritical relation, consistently in delivery reconciliation. Alternatively reject mixed-gas hydraulic delivery until qualified. Add a fixed-temperature composition sweep against an independent gas-flow limit; checking `valve_flow` against itself cannot detect this error.
