## R1 — Kinetics API still documents the removed quadrature and an overbroad accuracy bound

- **severity:** low
- **area:** contract
- **file:line:** crates/bwr-core/src/kinetics.rs:144; crates/bwr-core/src/kinetics.rs:14
- **wrong:** `advance_integrating` still says each accepted substep contributes a trapezoid and claims accuracy well under 1%, although round10 changes the accumulation to `self.n * h` at line 165. The decay-cap comment also retains the old half-cap area-excess explanation. The corrected endpoint rule has an exact scalar prompt-decay area only in the limit of integrating the entire decay: for `n'=-a*n`, its finite-window area is `n0/a * (1-(1+a*h)^(-N))`, versus the exact `n0/a * (1-exp(-a*N*h))`. A same-state independent matrix-exponential check of the first 0.001 s of the prior -0.020 drop gives 22,550.483958 J generated and 629.848961 J to coolant; production gives 21,575.714610 J and 607.409323 J, errors of -4.323% and -3.563%. Refining the internal step through 1 microsecond outer calls reduces these to -0.0230% and -0.0203%. Separately, the existing 0.010 rapid-rise fixture has 1.309% generated-energy error over 0.05 s, within its actual 2% regression tolerance but not the API's unqualified sub-1% wording.
- **consequence:** Callers and future numerical work can rely on the wrong integration rule and mistake successful full-drop energy checks for uniform finite-window accuracy. This is not evidence of a material error in the stock 0.05 s plant trajectories: the same drop integrated over 0.05 s now has only -0.000631% generated-energy error and +0.0693% coolant-energy error.
- **fix:** Update both doc comments to describe the backward-Euler endpoint integral, state that the scalar exact-area result is an infinite-time limit, and document the measured accuracy scope and regression tolerances instead of a blanket sub-1% guarantee. Preserve the distinction between time-distribution error and total prompt-drop area error.
