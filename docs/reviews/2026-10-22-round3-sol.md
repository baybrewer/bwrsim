## R1 — Liquid main-line delivery receives steam-turbine specific work
- **severity:** high
- **area:** physics
- **file:line:** crates/bwr-core/src/sim.rs:504
- **wrong:** Round3 explicitly passes displaced compressed liquid into the steam line (`sim.rs:442`), but turbine work still ignores inlet enthalpy/phase (`bop.rs:257`). Isolated valid EOS fixture: FullPower with M=1.002*V*rho_f(200 C), consistent M/U/EOS, sealed gas-free head; one accepted 0.05 s step delivers 242.436587 kg/s at h=853319.631 J/kg and produces 155.674979 MW, or 642126.591 J/kg. Independent IF97 expansion from 200 C/3.834878 MPa to the actual initial condenser pressure, 5.609667 kPa, at constant entropy permits at most 145330.789 J/kg. The modeled work exceeds the reversible limit by 4.42 times. The step reports no model error.
- **consequence:** A water-solid discharge can generate physically impossible shaft power and cool the condenser through an entropy-violating transfer even while first-law bookkeeping closes.
- **fix:** Pass actual inlet enthalpy and pressure into an inlet-state-aware expansion model and bound work by an isentropic enthalpy drop times efficiency. If liquid ingestion is outside scope, reject it transactionally or explicitly trip/isolate the turbine before assigning steam work/extraction; retain separately modeled liquid bypass discharge. Add the 200 C liquid fixture with an independent work limit.

## R2 — Accepted flows retain a chest pressure solved for the rejected request
- **severity:** medium
- **area:** physics
- **file:line:** crates/bwr-core/src/sim.rs:499
- **wrong:** Only TCV/bypass mass flows are scaled to accepted supply; p_chest remains from `bop.rs:79` at requested flow and is consumed by turbine work, the next EHC scan and MSIV protection. Vapor-limited FullPower, M=V*rho_f(T)*(1-1e-8), consistent M/U/EOS, dt=0.05 s: delivered flow=0.002000707498 kg/s and retained chest=7.004448771 MPa. The line relation at that delivered flow gives 7.202308719 MPa; the unchanged TCV relation at the same flow gives only 0.101354544 MPa. There is no common hydraulic state for the committed quantities.
- **consequence:** Flow telemetry is fixed, but downstream work/control/protection still consumes an incompatible pressure; supply limitation can alter trips and shaft response incorrectly.
- **fix:** Solve supply-limited delivery, chest pressure and valve flow together, with chest storage or an explicit supported supply-limit boundary. Commit the resulting pressure with the flows. Do not merely substitute delivered flow into the line pressure-drop formula, which would leave the valve relation inconsistent. Add a limiting test for both hydraulic relations and the resulting turbine/protection inputs.

## R3 — Browser core boiling now uses vapor partial pressure instead of mechanical pressure
- **severity:** medium
- **area:** physics
- **file:line:** ui/src/sim/mock.ts:546
- **wrong:** Round3 subtracts headGasPressureMpa before evaluating core void onset, and also uses that partial-pressure saturation for inlet subcooling at `:744`. Surface vapor equilibrium uses partial pressure; bubbles inside the liquid must oppose total mechanical pressure, as the engine explicitly implements at `vessel.rs:800`. Isolated mock cold-load -> seal -> T=150 C, thermal power=1 MW: total pressure=0.603250580 MPa, air partial=0.134346802 MPa, yet updateVoids reports 2.576641% core void and flow=690.645906 kg/s. Independent IF97 boiling temperature at total pressure is 159.043706 C; the core adds only 1.448 kJ/kg, far below the roughly 38 kJ/kg bulk subcooling deficit.
- **consequence:** Sealed gas-bearing heatup produces premature core boiling, void feedback and natural circulation, and understates inlet subcooling relative to the engine.
- **fix:** Keep partial-pressure saturation for head-space vapor properties, but compute core boiling and inlet subcooling from total pressure and the actual inlet/core heat rise. Add a sealed-gas subcooled-core case and a no-gas boundary case against engine semantics.
