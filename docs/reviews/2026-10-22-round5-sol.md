## R1 — Liquid valve correction can reduce flow when driving pressure increases

- **severity:** medium
- **area:** physics
- **file:line:** crates/bwr-core/src/bop.rs:114
- **wrong:** Multiplying the pressure-linear steam conductance by `rho_in/rho_vapour(p)` does not produce a liquid valve law. With fully open MSIVs, TCV=0.01, bypass shut and compressed water at 40 C, raising inlet pressure from 3.834878 to 7.2 MPa changes flow from 142.423892 to 138.415555 kg/s, despite nearly doubling valve differential pressure. Independent IF97 densities are 993.851836 and 995.308422 kg/m3, so neither density change nor flashing explains the reversal: even the atmospheric outlet is above this water's vapor pressure. Line losses are only 696.6/657.0 Pa. The inferred fixed-opening discharge area changes from 0.00165343 to 0.00116445 m2. A turbulent liquid restriction instead has mass flow proportional to the square root of density times pressure drop; see the manufacturer's [differential-pressure flow relation](https://www.spiraxsarco.com/learn-about-steam/flowmetering/instrumentation). The regression at `bop.rs:1220` repeats the implemented vapor-density scaling, then bisects the same equation, so it cannot reject this physical error.
- **consequence:** Accepted flooded discharge, chest pressure, level recovery and isolation response remain quantitatively wrong. The 200 C plant fixture now accepts about 6355.96 kg/s and 5.424 GW of condenser inlet enthalpy without any independent liquid-valve qualification.
- **fix:** Give liquid a calibrated restriction/Cv law using actual upstream/downstream pressure and liquid density; enforce cavitation/flashing/choking limits when applicable and solve the line and valves together. Retain the corrected inverse-density line resistance. Add the nonflashing 40 C pressure-monotonicity and square-root limiting checks independently of the implementation; reject unsupported flashing paths transactionally until modeled.

## R2 — Cold gas-bearing main-line discharge retains all noncondensables

- **severity:** medium
- **area:** physics
- **file:line:** crates/bwr-core/src/vessel.rs:690
- **wrong:** Cold main-line flow is now enabled from total pressure, but the port removes only water vapor while the sealed EOS at `vessel.rs:744` keeps `gas_mass` constant. In a consistent sealed 90 C, 180000 kg water, 100 kg air vessel with open MSIVs and TCV=0.1, one second discharges 0.312963206/0.312963016/0.312962921 kg of water at dt=0.05/0.025/0.0125 s while air remains exactly 100 kg. The initial homogeneous dome gas has about 3.62 kg air per kg vapor: retaining all air would require a selective separator that this port does not model. Integrating that instantaneous composition against the accepted vapor flow implies approximately 1.131663 kg air coflow in each check, not zero. This is a composition consistency check, not a prediction from a complete mixture hydraulic solve.
- **consequence:** Pressure decay and gas purging are wrong during gas-bearing discharge; the condenser receives pure-water enthalpy and no corresponding noncondensable load. Water-only mass/energy residuals cannot detect this error.
- **fix:** Transport water vapor and noncondensables together from dome composition, debit vessel gas inventory, and pass mixture density/enthalpy and gas loading to downstream models. Include gas inventory in acceptance and rollback. Until supported, isolate/reject gas-bearing main-line delivery explicitly rather than accepting selective vapor withdrawal.

## R3 — Gas-free cold core boils at dome pressure despite the overlying water head

- **severity:** medium
- **area:** physics
- **file:line:** crates/bwr-core/src/vessel.rs:812
- **wrong:** Returning `sat_p(eos.p)` applies dome pressure to every axial core cell. For a sealed, gas-free 90 C vessel at normal level, dome pressure is 70182.36 Pa and the core top lies 12 m below the surface. Even a void-free hydrostatic estimate puts core-top pressure at 183817.994 Pa, with IF97 saturation temperature 117.564598 C and saturated-liquid enthalpy 493437.511 J/kg. A 500 kg/s, 1 MW core receives 376968.4 J/kg and exits at only 378968.4 J/kg, so it remains far below boiling even at the least-pressurized core elevation. Nevertheless, the axial model predicts 26.462754% average equilibrium void and 0.000876209 exit quality. Accepted vessel steps report 26.459327/26.461040/26.461897% equilibrium core void at dt=0.05/0.025/0.0125 s. The former atmospheric floor gives exactly zero in this fixture; using the conservative core-top pressure also gives zero.
- **consequence:** Round 5 enables substantial spurious cold-core void feedback and level swell, with the error persisting as timestep decreases. Using total dome pressure fixes the earlier air-pressure defect but does not supply the local mechanical pressure required for bubble formation.
- **fix:** Evaluate saturation along the core using local pressure, including the overlying liquid/mixture hydrostatic head and appropriate flow losses; use that pressure consistently in the axial boiling and chimney calculations and initialization. Add this deeply subcooled gas-free limiting fixture and startup/refinement checks; a temperature-based pressure floor is not a substitute.

## R4 — Atmospheric downstream boundary blocks steam delivery into condenser vacuum

- **severity:** medium
- **area:** physics
- **file:line:** crates/bwr-core/src/bop.rs:89
- **wrong:** Both main-line branches use `p-P_ATM` and have no condenser backpressure input. An isolated saturated-vapor solve at 90 C and 70182.36 Pa with fully open MSIVs/bypass returns exactly zero flow, although a normal evacuated `Condenser::new(true)` is at 2639.215 Pa. There is a positive 67543.145 Pa driving difference and an open path to vacuum; pressure below atmospheric does not imply zero delivery. This remains after removal of the 100 C main-line gate.
- **consequence:** Low-pressure cooldown and draining cannot discharge to an available condenser below atmospheric vessel pressure. The flow cutoff is attached to an unrelated atmospheric boundary rather than actual downstream pressure, so low-pressure trajectories and equal-pressure limits are wrong.
- **fix:** Pass the actual receiving pressure into the hydraulic solve and use compressible valve relations with appropriate choked/unchoked transitions. Enforce zero forward flow at equal or reversed actual pressures, and add a below-atmospheric upstream/evacuated-condenser positive-flow check plus the equal-pressure limit. Carry the same boundary into supply-limited pressure reconciliation.
